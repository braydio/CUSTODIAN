# Lattice, Archive Engine, and Memory Glass Doctrine

**Project:** CUSTODIAN
**Status:** canonical / active
**Authority:** Addendum to `design/03_world/LATTICE_DOCTRINE.md` and `design/03_world/RECIPROCAL_CONTINUITY_DOCTRINE.md`
**Purpose:** Define the relationship between the Lattice, Archive Engines, Memory Glass, and continuity operations.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document hardens the relationship between three core CUSTODIAN concepts:

* **The Lattice** — the protected continuity field / survivable reality pocket.
* **Archive Engines** — the infrastructure that inflates, reinforces, monitors, and maintains the Lattice.
* **Memory Glass** — the high-integrity mnemonic / telemetry substrate inside Archive Engines.

This document exists to prevent term drift.

The Lattice is not the engine.
The Archive Engine is not the Lattice.
Memory Glass is not generic data storage.

Together, they explain how a reality pocket holds together, how it fails, and why fragments of broken Memory Glass can contain sampled states from multiple actual continuity origins.

---

## 2. Canonical Relationship

### 2.1 The Lattice

The **Lattice** is the temporal/existential container that makes a bounded reality pocket survivable.

A Lattice is not a planet, colony, shield dome, simulation, or permanent world-state. It is a temporary continuity field imposed around a slice of place, history, witness, and meaning.

A Lattice:

* holds a bounded pocket of reality coherent enough to inhabit;
* preserves causal and historical continuity within that pocket;
* resists Pale intrusion for a finite time;
* depends on Archive infrastructure for reinforcement;
* eventually collapses.

Lattices may couple compatible continuity states. A Lattice aperture is therefore both transport infrastructure and a possible reciprocal continuity path. This is the physical basis of the Reciprocal Continuity Law.

### 2.2 Archive Engine

The **Archive Engine** is the infrastructure that inflates, reinforces, monitors, and maintains the Lattice.

Archive Engines are not simple computers. They are continuity infrastructure: field controller, stabilizer, diagnostics system, and authority record in one system.

An Archive Engine:

* controls Lattice field geometry;
* maintains anchors and field coherence;
* monitors phase and state coherence;
* classifies continuity signatures;
* tracks route history;
* detects foreign-state interference;
* operates containment boundaries;
* stores diagnostics and authority records;
* fails when field integrity is exceeded.

Archive Engines do not permanently save worlds. They buy coherence.

### 2.3 Memory Glass

**Memory Glass** is a high-integrity mnemonic / telemetry substrate used by Archive Engines.

It is not ordinary glass and not ordinary memory. It is a physical medium used by Archive Engines to preserve:

* historical logs;
* field telemetry;
* continuity fingerprints;
* route topology;
* authority records;
* containment orders;
* cross-continuity observations;
* calibration data.

A shard of Memory Glass can contain incompatible histories because the Engine sampled **multiple actual states** from different continuity origins, not because each history was trying to become metaphysically authoritative.

Memory Glass is therefore part archive matter, part telemetry substrate, part forensic medium, and part calibration record.

## 3. How the System Fails

### 3.1 Lattice Collapse

When a Lattice's field coherence degrades below operational threshold:

* the reality pocket loses coherence;
* Pale intrusion accelerates;
* continuity boundaries destabilize;
* cross-continuity drift increases;
* the field eventually collapses entirely.

### 3.2 Archive Engine Failure

When an Archive Engine fails:

* field geometry control is lost;
* coherence monitoring stops;
* containment boundaries degrade;
* foreign-state interference goes undetected;
* the Lattice collapses on a shorter timeline.

### 3.3 Memory Glass Damage

When Memory Glass is damaged:

* continuity fingerprints become unreadable;
* route topology is lost;
* authority records become disputed;
* cross-continuity observations become orphaned;
* diagnostics become fragmentary.

A shard of damaged Memory Glass can contain perfectly coherent records from a continuity that no longer exists locally. The records are real. The continuity they describe may be gone, or may be an adjacent state that was once coupled.

## 4. Gameplay Role

### 4.1 Memory Glass as Upgrade Material

Memory Glass shards are the primary material used to upgrade Archive Engine systems. Each shard carries:

* continuity fingerprint data;
* route topology fragments;
* calibration records;
* authority residues.

Using a shard to upgrade an Engine means integrating its sampled data into the Engine's active diagnostics.

### 4.2 Memory Glass as Archive Reconstruction

Recovering Memory Glass shards allows the player to reconstruct:

* what continuity states were present at a site;
* what routes were active before collapse;
* what authority records existed;
* what containment orders were in effect.

### 4.3 Memory Glass as Anomaly Analysis

Memory Glass fragments help classify continuity anomalies by providing:

* cross-continuity observations;
* continuity-origin signatures;
* route history;
* field telemetry from the moment of collapse.

### 4.4 Memory Glass as Sealed Knowledge

Some Memory Glass is sealed under authority orders. Opening it requires:

* appropriate Custodian authority;
* correct operational context;
* sometimes, a specific key or authorization sequence.

Sealed Memory Glass may contain containment orders, classified route data, or Null Warrant authority records.
