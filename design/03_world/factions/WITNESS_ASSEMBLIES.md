# Witness Assemblies

> **Classification:** Major civic and social polity family

## Identity and Material Basis

Witness Assemblies rebuild civic personhood where records, designations, and
machine histories are incomplete or contradictory. Their material basis is the
need to recognize displaced people, inherit obligations, reunite households,
settle identity disputes, and make social continuity governable.

## Organization and Doctrine

Assemblies include neighborhood witness circles, traveling attestation panels,
kinship federations, restorative courts, and civic registries. Their doctrine is
that a person is more than the evidence a damaged machine can authenticate.

## What It Gets Right

Social testimony contains real knowledge. Communities can recognize people and
obligations that formal archives erase, duplicate, or reject.

## Failure Mode

Recognition can become patronage. Insiders may monopolize credibility, memory
may harden into dogma, and unfamiliar people may be denied personhood because
nobody powerful will speak for them.

## Domain Policy

Preserve communities through displacement, record layered testimony, maintain
rights across contradictory histories, and treat evacuation as social rather
than merely logistical continuity.

## Custodian Relationship

The Custodian is a person authenticated strongly by machines and weakly by
living memory. Assemblies force the setting to ask whether designation proves
identity, authority, both, or neither.

## Environmental Grammar

Public testimony circles, relationship maps, memorial walls, duplicate-name
registers, household tokens, accessible hearing rooms, annotated machine
records, and visible dissent attached to official decisions.

## Pre-Combat and Noncombat Behavior

Witnesses interview, compare accounts, escort disputed people, conduct public
hearings, and shield those under review. Conflict begins through refusal,
protest, or custody disputes before violence.

## Gameplay Opportunities

A machine rejects a locally known citizen; refugees lack continuity provenance;
two valid histories claim one identity; testimony would admit a possible hazard;
Custodian designation contradicts everybody who remembers the event.

## Internal Variation

Buried-Kin societies, cosmopolitan civic assemblies, mobile refugee panels,
formal restorative courts, and exclusionary lineage blocs all exist.

## Relations

Cordon contests proof; Charter contests standing; Drawdown contests who receives
support; Recovery contests ownership of abandoned goods; Orraic Orders often
ally in rescue; Fieldworks must decide whether its load maps include social ties.
