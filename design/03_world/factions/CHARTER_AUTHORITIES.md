# Charter Authorities

> **Classification:** Major polity family

## Identity and Material Basis

Charter Authorities organize standing: who may operate a pump, tax a route,
maintain an anchor, settle a dispute, or inherit responsibility for shared
systems. Their material base is infrastructure whose safe use requires durable
rights and obligations.

## Organization and Doctrine

They include municipal charters, cooperative trusts, route courts, hereditary
stewardships, licensed operators, and federated utilities. Their doctrine is
that access without accountable stewardship becomes seizure and neglect.

## What It Gets Right

Infrastructure needs rules that outlast one emergency. Clear obligations can
prevent both strongmen and transient rescuers from stripping common systems.

## Failure Mode

Standing becomes ownership theater. Leaseholder tendencies monetize dependency,
inherit dead claims, price people out of necessities, and confuse machine
acceptance with justice.

## Domain Policy

Assign maintainers, record rights, arbitrate competing uses, and preserve a
legible chain of responsibility across political turnover.

## Custodian Relationship

Ancient systems may recognize the Custodian above a charter that has governed
for generations. Authorities may seek ratification, constitutional limits, or
open resistance to that technically valid but socially explosive precedence.

## Environmental Grammar

Public claim boards, service seals, access schedules, court alcoves, maintained
locks, duty rosters, metered distribution, and visible appeals procedures—or
their predatory imitation.

## Pre-Combat and Noncombat Behavior

Officials inspect credentials, negotiate access, collect duties, mediate queues,
and post notices. Enforcement escalates through claims and warnings before force.

## Gameplay Opportunities

An old door accepts Custodian command over local law; rival charters claim one
anchor; a community maintains a system it cannot legally access; exercising
authority solves the objective and starts a constitutional crisis.

## Internal Variation

Commons charters, municipal authorities, commercial concessions, lineage trusts,
and Leaseholder extremes fight over what legitimate stewardship means.

## Relations

Fieldworks wants practical access; Drawdown wants enforceable allocation;
Cordon invokes emergency standing; Recovery bargains for salvage rights;
Witness contests paper identity; Orraic Orders test law against human need.
