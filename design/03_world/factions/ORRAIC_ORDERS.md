# Orraic Orders

> **Classification:** Major polity family; plural traditions, never one church

## Identity and Material Basis

Orraic Orders are networks of rescue houses, hospices, route chapels, mutual-aid
crews, ethical schools, and sworn service communities shaped by the remembered
dilemma of Orra: procedure matters, and systems must still answer to people.
They are not one universal religion and do not share one theology.

## Organization and Doctrine

Some Orders are devotional, others secular, philosophical, civic, or descended
from emergency services. They organize around vows and practices rather than a
central hierarchy. The shared question is when duty requires interrupting the
system one was assigned to preserve.

## What It Gets Right

Optimization can erase the person in front of it. Rescue, hospitality, and
refusal can be necessary forms of institutional competence.

## Failure Mode

Orders can glorify sacrifice, demand impossible rescues, fragment into doctrinal
feuds, or turn Orra's dilemma into a ritual answer applied without context.

## Domain Policy

Keep refuge routes open, preserve access to care, intervene for stranded people,
and judge infrastructure by whom it serves.

## Custodian Relationship

Many Orders regard the Custodian as a duty-bearing person, not a sacred office.
They may shelter, challenge, or obstruct the Custodian when ancient procedure
conflicts with immediate human need.

## Environmental Grammar

Waystations, repairable bells, rescue caches, lists of missing people, shared
kitchens, marked refuge routes, field clinics, vow texts, and practical ritual
objects whose forms vary by Order.

## Pre-Combat and Noncombat Behavior

Members render aid, search ruins, negotiate passage, tend the displaced, and
ring or relay warnings. They can oppose the player without attacking and fight
only when a rescue, refuge, or vow is endangered.

## Gameplay Opportunities

Divert from containment for a rescue; choose who receives a limited evacuation
window; confront an Order repeating Orra's dilemma badly; accept help whose vow
creates a later obligation.

## Internal Variation

Ash-Bell listeners, secular service orders, militant rescuers, hospice networks,
and contemplative schools may share almost no metaphysics. Pale Bell is one
local Ash-Bell tradition, not the definition of Orraic identity.

## Relations

Cordon is its sharpest mercy/safety conflict; Drawdown disputes the last rescue;
Fieldworks provides practical allies; Witness Assemblies share human recognition;
Charter tests law against obligation; Recovery can be rescuer or grave robber.
