## Core Thesis

CUSTODIAN's central mystery concerns a civilization that learned to establish stable access between adjacent continuities.

The old Lattice civilization expanded through those connections without fully understanding their reciprocal nature.

Every continuity reached represented not only a destination, but a potential route back.

The Severing destroyed or deliberately amputated much of that network after a continuity hazard began propagating through it.

The player does not initially know what crossed, whether anything intelligent crossed at all, or whether the Severing was disaster, attack, containment, or all three.

The world that remains is filled with damaged Lattice pockets, cross-continuity debris, deliberately severed routes, institutional cover-ups, and people living inside the consequences.

---

## Layer 1 — The Severing (Public Catastrophe)

This is the broadly known name for the collapse. Most people, factions, terminals, degraded records, and Hub systems refer to the great collapse as **The Severing**.

Player understanding at this layer:

- The interstellar / continuity network collapsed
- Worlds became isolated
- Old institutions shattered
- Pale boundaries spread
- Lattice infrastructure failed

The Severing is treated as a historical catastrophe. Everyone knows something went wrong. Most people do not know the specific cause. They know the results: severed routes, spreading Pale, failing Lattice pockets, lost colonies, broken supply chains, and an unstable cosmos.

---

## Layer 2 — Continuity Contamination

The player discovers that contradictions in the world are not merely inaccurate records. Some are genuine continuity artifacts.

Player understanding at this layer:

- Some impossible objects are genuinely foreign
- Some contradictory witnesses come from different continuities
- Some structures overlap alternate histories
- Some old routes were deliberately destroyed

This layer is where the game begins to feel wrong. Not "someone lied" but "two real things cannot occupy the same space." Objects that do not belong. Records that are all true in their originating continuity. Architecture that does not match local history. People who remember events that happened in adjacent states.

---

## Layer 3 — The Return-Path Problem

Restricted historical knowledge. The old civilization knew about the reciprocal nature of continuity access and attempted to manage it.

Player understanding at this layer:

- Old civilization knowingly accessed adjacent continuities
- Lattice paths were not fundamentally one-way
- Old authorities discovered return-path risk
- Containment programs began before the Severing

This is where the player learns that the catastrophe was not ignorance. The old authorities knew. They built containment programs. They created the Null Warrant Office. They tried to manage the return-path problem. It was not enough, or they did not act in time, or someone decided the risk was acceptable.

---

## Layer 4 — Null Warrant

Late-game institutional layer. The Null Warrant Office was the old-world authority that handled continuity hazards, dangerous Lattice research, route quarantine, and classified containment.

Questions this layer raises:

- What did they know?
- What did they destroy?
- Did they initiate the Severing?
- Did they save anyone?
- What was returning?

The Null Warrant Office does not need to be fully explained. It functions as evidence that the old civilization understood the hazard better than the public record suggests, and chose containment over disclosure.

Ash-Bell makes that institutional terror visible. Before the Protocol, still
humanoid figures recurred around the Lower Quarter in physically inaccessible
places. Null Warrant recognized enough to withdraw, quarantine transit, and
eventually seal West Gate when it could no longer verify the continuity origin
of the evacuation flow. The sightings are historical canon; their identity,
intent, and relationship to `NON-RECIPIENT` or the Severing are not.

---

## Layer 5 — Reserved Cosmic Mystery

Do not answer.

- What first demonstrated reciprocal traversal?
- What was Null Warrant containing?
- Was the Severing deliberate?
- How many continuity routes survived?
- Are active Lattice restorations recreating dangerous paths?
- Is the Pale related to the original hazard?
- Has anything been following restored routes?

These questions can sit in the dark for a long time. The game does not need to answer them. The player needs to feel that the answers exist and that someone, somewhere, might already know them.

---

## The Ash-Bell Place in the Hierarchy

Ash-Bell is not Layer 2 of the universal apocalypse.

Ash-Bell is a historically important case study inside the wider continuity catastrophe. It is the player's most intimate demonstration of the same physics that caused the Severing. Huge cosmic premise, human-sized tragedy.

The Ash-Bell Unarrival, Precentor Orra, the Open Interval, and the Ninth Answer are the human face of the return-path problem. The player encounters them not as abstract cosmology but as a ruined chapel, a broken bell, a wrong basin, a ghost procession, and a phrase that means more than it says.

The tragedy begins before the missed Answer: figures at impossible distances,
Null Warrant officers afraid in public, a transit corridor failing after
quarantine diversion, and a gate closed against a crowd it could physically
have passed but could no longer safely identify. Orra turns back at that moral
fracture. White thread's first meaning is emergency identity and custody—**Name
first. Wrist second.** Its later sacred meaning grows from that practical wound.

## Knowledge Delivery Rules

The player should discover lore through:

- Environmental evidence (layout, objects, damage, signage)
- Machine remnants and readouts
- Faction behavior and ritual
- Terminal records
- NPC dialogue
- Inspect text

The player should never be told the full cosmology at once. Each layer should feel like a more dangerous interpretation of the previous layer.

Provenance is never presented as supernatural. It is always forensic: where did this come from, through what route, with what evidence chain.
