# CUSTODIAN Player VFX Design Lock
## Black-Gold Afterimage with Amber Vector Impact Language

## 1. Core Intent

The CUSTODIAN player VFX language should feel:

- **controlled**
- **precise**
- **ominous**
- **kinetic**
- **premium**
- **readable at gameplay scale**

The Operator’s effects are **not elemental fire**, **not magic smoke**, and **not generic anime slash trails**.

They are best understood as:

> **compressed motion pressure, dark afterimage residue, and tightly focused amber-gold impact energy**

Player should feel like trained, dangerous figure whose motion disturbs space in disciplined, stylized way.

The VFX must support animation, not compete with it.

---

# 2. Primary Style Statement

> **CUSTODIAN player VFX are dark kinetic afterimages traced with compressed amber-gold energy. Motion is expressed with clean directional geometry. Confirmed impacts briefly expose white-hot core and fracture into few sharp gold particles. Effects remain tightly bound to actor silhouette and never obscure animation.**

This is master style sentence.
If any future VFX idea contradicts it, effect should be revised.

---

# 3. Visual Identity

## 3.1 Primary read

Visual stack should read in this order:

1. **dark afterimage / motion residue**
2. **thin amber-gold vector contour or streak**
3. **small white-hot impact core**
4. **minimal sharp particulate breakup**

That means the VFX are **anchored to silhouette first**, and only become bright at moment of contact.

---

## 3.2 Palette lock

### Core palette roles

- **Near-black / charcoal**
 - used for afterimage residue
 - semi-subtle, silhouette-adjacent
 - should feel like shadow pressure, not smoke

- **Amber-gold**
 - main energy body
 - should match the Operator’s trim and eye energy
 - primary identity color

- **Orange fringe**
 - limited accent around active edges
 - used to warm amber and add energy separation

- **White-hot highlight**
 - used only at strongest contact moment
 - sparse
 - should feel earned, not constant

### Palette rule

The VFX must visually belong to the Operator’s existing identity:

- black armor
- gold trim
- amber eye glow

Effect should feel like it emerges from that same system.

### Prohibited palette drift

Do **not** let player VFX drift toward:

- red fire
- yellow cartoon lightning
- blue sci-fi plasma
- purple magic
- soft rainbow bloom
- white spam

If effect gets too bright or too colorful, it stops feeling like CUSTODIAN.

---

# 4. Shape Language

## 4.1 Main shapes

Approved shape vocabulary is:

- thin directional streaks
- tapered wedges
- crescent cuts
- contour-hugging arcs
- compact rings / halos for major impact
- sparse angular particle chips

These shapes should feel **purposeful** and **vector-like**, not soft or gaseous.

---

## 4.2 Forbidden shapes

Avoid:

- large puffy fireballs
- fluffy smoke clouds
- oversized explosion stars
- random spark showers
- thick glow noodles
- giant anime energy waves
- screen-filling slashes

The Operator is not spraying magic across screen.
The VFX should feel compressed and intentional.

---

# 5. Motion Language

## 5.1 Global motion principle

> **effect should describe motion before it describes power.**

That means the VFX should first communicate:

- where limb moved
- how fast it moved
- what direction strike traveled
- where impact happened

Only after that should it communicate force.

---

## 5.2 Timing structure

Most player attack VFX should follow this structure:

### Anticipation
- small
- may be nearly invisible
- tiny spark, short contour flicker, or minimal afterimage hint

### Active travel
- clean directional streak or contour
- follows strike path
- readable shape, not visual noise

### Contact
- smallest frame with highest clarity
- white-hot point or compact impact knot
- strongest amber concentration
- optional compact fracture burst

### Recovery
- quick taper
- few broken gold fragments or diminishing edge traces
- no lingering noise cloud

---

## 5.3 Residency rule

Effect should exist only as long as it contributes useful information.

It should **not** linger because “more particles looks cooler.”

Short and clean beats long and noisy.

---

# 6. Hierarchy and Readability

## 6.1 Character-first rule

> **character must remain visually more important than effect.**

At all times:

- body silhouette must stay readable
- striking limb must remain legible
- pose should not disappear inside effect
- player should be able to read direction and timing instantly

The VFX is amplifier, not star.

---

## 6.2 Contact emphasis rule

Only the **contact moment** should be allowed to peak in brightness and density.

This gives action cadence and prevents whole chain from flattening into constant noise.

---

## 6.3 Escalation rule

Effects within combo should escalate by:

- shape breadth
- impact clarity
- brightness peak
- fragmentation amount

They should **not** escalate mainly by becoming huge.

Bigger is not automatically better.
Clearer and more forceful is better.

---

# 7. Afterimage Rule

The **dark afterimage** is signature element that makes this feel like CUSTODIAN rather than generic amber hit sparks.

## 7.1 What it is

Brief, silhouette-adjacent dark residue that implies motion displacement.

Examples:

- ghosted forearm
- partial torso sweep contour
- body-edge echo through turning strike
- shallow void-ring or dark motion echo before impact

## 7.2 What it is not

It is not:

- smoke
- blur
- shadow clone spam
- whole extra opaque body
- second character

Afterimage should be:

- subtle
- tightly localized
- short-lived
- mostly subordinate to real pose

---

# 8. Impact Rule

Impact peaks must be:

- compact
- sharp
- directional when appropriate
- brightest at center
- quickly collapsing after contact

## 8.1 White-hot core usage

White should appear primarily as:

- contact point
- tiny contact seam
- compact burst center
- small knot in flare

White should almost never dominate whole effect.

---

## 8.2 Breakup behavior

After impact, effects should break into:

- 2 to 6 small angular particles
- small chipped streaks
- fading edge traces

Do not use giant particle sprays.

---

# 9. Fast Attack Combo Application

This section locks how style should behave for current unarmed four-link chain.

---

## 9.1 Fast 01
### Role
Quick opener / jab

### VFX identity
- smallest and cleanest in set
- subtle directional jab spark
- tiny crescent or thrust line
- minimal afterimage
- small contact accent

### Feel
Fast probing hit, not power move.

### Rule
If Fast 01 looks “cooler” than Fast 03 or Fast 04, it is wrong.

---

## 9.2 Fast 02
### Role
Committed cross

### VFX identity
- longer forward thrust shape
- clearer linear travel
- slightly stronger contact burst
- still narrow and disciplined
- light dark-afterimage shoulder/arm echo acceptable

### Feel
More committed than Fast 01, still fast and controlled.

### Rule
Should read more like a **driving line** than sweep.

---

## 9.3 Fast 03
### Role
Rotational strike / hook-like sweep

### VFX identity
- broader curved motion contour
- more visible afterimage/body arc
- stronger amber ribbon
- contact flare at turning apex
- more breakup than 01 or 02

### Feel
This is first strike that should feel meaningfully “heavy.”

### Rule
Player should feel rotation.

---

## 9.4 Fast 04
### Role
Palm finisher / terminal hit

### VFX identity
- strongest and most distinct of set
- compact radial or halo-like palm burst
- strongest dark-to-gold contrast
- brief white-hot center
- controlled ring or pressure-wave accent
- modest ember fracture on release

### Feel
Decisive terminal punctuation mark.

### Rule
Fast 04 gets biggest payoff, but must still stay compact enough that body pose remains readable.

---

# 10. Broader Action Mapping

This language should also extend consistently to other player actions.

## 10.1 Dash / burst movement
- dark body-adjacent afterimage
- thin gold trailing edge
- minimal white
- emphasis on displacement, not impact

## 10.2 Parry / deflect
- sharper, cleaner, more geometric
- higher white accent than normal movement
- compact ring or notch burst
- metallic precision, not explosive violence

## 10.3 Heavy attack
- thicker vector arcs
- more pronounced body afterimage
- broader fracture breakup
- brighter contact knot
- still controlled, not oversized fantasy slash

## 10.4 Critical / execution / special payoff
- strongest use of system
- slightly longer linger permitted
- afterimage + concentrated gold contour + fracture ring
- still never devolves into giant flame spam

---

# 11. Technical Art Rules

## 11.1 Pixel treatment
- crisp pixel art only
- clean nearest-neighbor readability
- no mushy subpixel blur look
- no painterly gradients that destroy readability

## 11.2 Alpha behavior
- transparent background
- clean alpha edges
- no dirty halo fog around effect
- no accidental semi-transparent haze blanket

## 11.3 Frame discipline
- effect must register cleanly inside its frame
- motion should follow strike path
- no erratic effect teleporting unrelated to body motion
- cleanup/fade frames should simplify, not become noisier

## 11.4 Density control
Per frame density should be deliberately bounded.

As rule:
- fewer, clearer pixels
- fewer, clearer particles
- one strong read per frame

Not:
- many tiny random events in every frame

---

# 12. Do / Don’t Lock

## Do
- keep effects silhouette-aware
- emphasize motion path
- use amber-gold as primary energy read
- reserve white for true impact
- use dark afterimage as identity marker
- keep effects compact
- escalate combo VFX by clarity and force
- let finisher have biggest payoff
- preserve gameplay readability above all

## Don’t
- turn attacks into fire magic
- fill frames with spark confetti
- use giant opaque slashes
- bury character in glow
- let every hit be equally loud
- use long smoky tails
- over-bloom effect
- make finisher so large that it obscures animation
- let VFX drift off-style from black/gold/amber identity

---

# 13. Approval Test

New player VFX set is on-style if, at glance:

1. It clearly belongs to black-armored amber-eyed Operator
2. It reads motion first and impact second
3. It preserves body readability
4. It uses dark afterimage and amber vector language coherently
5. Contact peaks feel earned and sharp
6. Combo escalation is obvious without becoming visually bloated

If it fails any of those, it is not yet locked.

---

# 14. Final Design Lock Statement

> **CUSTODIAN player VFX use a Black-Gold Afterimage / Amber Vector language. Motion is traced by subtle dark afterimage residue and disciplined amber-gold directional contours. Impacts peak in compact white-hot contact knots, then fracture into few sharp gold particles. Effects stay tightly bound to actor silhouette, remain readable at gameplay scale, and never overpower animation.**
