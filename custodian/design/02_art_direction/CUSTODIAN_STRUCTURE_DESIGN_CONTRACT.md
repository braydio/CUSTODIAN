# CUSTODIAN STRUCTURE DESIGN CONTRACT

**Status:** review
**Scope:** all interactable and environmental man-made structures in CUSTODIAN
**Purpose:** define a stable descriptive language for structures so future asset creation stays in-theme, readable, and production-usable.
**Anchor implementation:** Field Fabricator Mk1 (Class A — Utility Node)

---

## 1. Core Thesis

A Custodian structure is:

> A fortified industrial machine or installation built by an organized ruined authority. It is weathered, functional, and readable first, with restrained holy-tech undertones, a dark steel-and-stone material language, limited teal operational light, and a clear gameplay-facing interaction side.

Custodian structures are:

* industrial
* fortified
* weathered
* functional
* austere
* institutionally designed
* ritual-adjacent without being shrine-dominant

They are not:

* generic glossy science-fiction buildings
* cathedral machines
* fantasy altars
* random scrap piles
* Warhammer-style skull saturation
* neon cyberpunk machinery
* hyper-detailed RTS concept-art blobs
* decoration-first architecture

---

## 2. Structure Design Pillars

Every Custodian structure should satisfy these 5 pillars.

### 1. Function before ornament

A player should be able to make a reasonable guess about what a structure does from:

* silhouette
* major mechanisms
* access face
* apertures
* tanks
* conduits
* emitters
* output surfaces
* consoles

Decorative identity comes second.

If a machine's function disappears when faction ornament is removed, redesign it.

---

### 2. Fortified utility

Preferred physical grammar:

* thick housings
* reinforced corners
* inset chambers
* armored rings
* heavy panel seams
* structural clamps
* retaining braces
* protected apertures
* recessed vents
* guarded lamps
* anchored bases
* shallow ramps or service lips

Common infrastructure should feel designed to continue functioning in a hostile environment.

---

### 3. Ruined authority

These are not improvised post-apocalyptic machines.

They were built by an organized authority with:

* repeatable engineering conventions
* consistent material usage
* standardized access logic
* disciplined insignia placement
* recognizable construction hierarchy

Damage, grime, corrosion, missing plating, repair scars, and age may obscure this order, but must not erase it.

---

### 4. Restrained holy-tech

Allowed:

* small authority seals
* Custodian insignia
* circular engineering motifs
* reliquary-like protective housings
* grave institutional geometry
* sparse worn brass
* subtle processional symmetry in important structures

Restricted:

* candles
* shrines
* giant heraldic monuments
* religious statuary
* ornate filigree
* oversized banners
* glowing runes with no mechanical purpose
* skull decoration as default faction shorthand

A utility structure should remain a machine.

---

### 5. Gameplay readability

Every structure must read at actual gameplay scale.

Evaluate at:

#### Long range

Player sees:

* structure category
* faction ownership
* overall operational state

#### Mid range

Player sees:

* front / approach side
* major functional component
* interactable status

#### Close range

Player sees:

* vents
* panels
* clamps
* conduits
* wear
* small insignia
* maintenance details

Do not approve an asset based only on enlarged concept-art inspection.

---

# 3. Silhouette Contract

## 3.1 Base silhouette rule

Every structure needs a **clear primary mass**, a **secondary support mass**, and at most **1 tertiary accent cluster**.

Example:

* primary mass: fabrication chamber
* secondary mass: utility/power housing
* tertiary accent: side tank or antenna

Do not build every structure as a full ring of equally-detailed junk.

---

## 3.2 Structure should read from 3 distances

### Long range

The player should understand:

* "this is a structure"
* "this belongs to Custodian"
* "this is interactable / important / industrial"

### Mid range

The player should understand:

* the front
* the likely interaction side
* the structure's class

### Close range

The player should see:

* panels
* wear
* vents
* clamps
* cables
* insignia
* state lights

---

## 3.3 Shape vocabulary

Preferred silhouettes:

* squat industrial blocks
* ring-chamber with front access
* compact utility towers
* anchored modules
* reinforced pads with housed cores
* heavy box + chamber + side support cluster

Avoid:

* spindly fragile shapes
* ultra-tall tower-dominant silhouettes for common utility structures
* over-wide cathedral footprints
* pure circles with no readable frontage
* giant smooth monoliths with no function cues

---

# 4. Material Contract

## 4.1 Primary materials

Custodian structures should primarily use:

* dark steel
* blackened iron
* cold stone-metal composite
* weathered gray-blue metal
* gunmetal
* worn alloy
* occasional bronze / brass trim

These are the **default** materials.

---

## 4.2 Material finish

Surface finish should be:

* matte to semi-matte
* chipped
* scuffed
* stained
* slightly corroded
* grime-settled in recesses

Avoid:

* glossy polished luxury finish
* chrome
* clean white sterile sci-fi shell
* cartoon rust overload
* random rainbow metals

---

## 4.3 Structural layering

Good structure surfaces have layers like:

* outer plate
* recessed seam
* inner brace
* vent/grill
* protected light strip
* anchored foot/base

This layered construction is key to the Custodian feel.

---

# 5. Color Contract

## 5.1 Base palette

Primary structure colors:

* charcoal
* slate gray
* cold steel blue-gray
* dark ash
* desaturated olive-gray in rare soft materials / tarps

Secondary accents:

* brass
* muted bronze
* worn gold only in small amounts

Energy / signal accent:

* restrained teal / cyan

---

## 5.2 Accent ratio

Use this approximate ratio:

* **80–88%** dark neutrals
* **8–15%** metal accent / trim / grime variation
* **2–6%** emissive teal/cyan

The glow should feel precious and controlled, not like a nightclub.

---

## 5.3 Color rejection rules

Do not use:

* bright saturated red as a base design language
* vivid neon green as a core accent
* purple core glow for ordinary Custodian infrastructure
* candy-blue sci-fi surfaces
* gold everywhere

---

# 6. Lighting Contract

## 6.1 Emissive usage

Lights must indicate:

* power
* active state
* operational chamber
* console readability
* status strips
* signal mast

Lights should not exist just to make the asset look cooler.

---

## 6.2 Emissive placement

Preferred:

* inside chambers
* in narrow slits
* in status bars
* under protected glass
* in recessed consoles
* in compact beacons

Avoid:

* giant glowing outlines
* full-edge neon tracing
* too many separate glows
* magical aura clouds around ordinary machines

---

## 6.3 State readability

Every important structure should have at least:

* **unpowered state**
* **idle powered state**
* **active / working state**

These should be visually distinct mainly through lighting and small mechanism changes, not by changing the whole design.

---

# 7. Construction Grammar

Every Custodian structure should be assembled from a repeatable visual grammar.

## 7.1 Approved parts language

Use combinations of:

* ring chambers
* armored box housings
* reinforced pylons
* pipe elbows
* vent stacks
* ribbed conduits
* front ramps
* service lips
* inset consoles
* side tanks
* panel latches
* braces
* clamps
* feet / stabilizers
* guarded beacons
* controlled insignia plates

---

## 7.2 Restricted detail types

Allowed in moderation:

* tarped crate-like attachments
* cable bundles
* small fans
* utility canisters
* compact signal antennas

Not allowed as dominant design:

* skull spam
* giant banners on all structures
* church windows
* ornate filigree
* huge ceremonial symbols
* random protrusions with no purpose

---

## 7.3 Insignia rule

Faction insignia should be:

* present
* small
* deliberate
* stamped or mounted

Good:

* one front plate
* one side tank
* one banner on a major structure

Bad:

* insignia on every panel face

---

# 8. Frontage / Interaction Contract

This is critical.

Every interactable structure must have a **readable interaction face**.

## Required interaction cues

At least 2 of the following:

* front ramp
* service lip
* output tray
* console
* access hatch
* bay opening
* central interaction plate
* inward-facing chamber opening

The player should know where to approach without needing trial and error.

---

# 9. Structure Classes

Use these classes to keep assets coherent.

## Class A — Utility Node

Examples:

* Field Fabricator
* Capacitor Bank
* Repair unit
* Sensor support hardware

Characteristics:

* compact
* immediately functional
* strong front face
* minimal ceremony

## Class B — Transit / Ingress

Examples:

* transfer interfaces
* ingress sites
* controlled gate machinery

Characteristics:

* stronger central focal geometry
* clearer destination symbolism
* moderate ceremonial undertone permitted

## Class C — Defensive / Authority

Examples:

* checkpoints
* defensive emplacements
* gate controls
* fortified command machinery

Characteristics:

* heavier frontal mass
* stronger defensive construction
* more institutional presence

## Class D — Strategic / Major

Examples:

* large foundries
* compound hubs
* authority-scale processing structures

Characteristics:

* increased symbolic weight
* larger silhouette budget
* greater architectural complexity
* holy-tech influence may increase, but function remains legible

---

# 10. Hard Rejection Rules

Reject an asset when it primarily reads as:

* generic sci-fi
* cathedral
* shrine
* altar
* random junk
* oversized monument
* UI icon
* visual-effects showcase
* unreadable detail soup
* faction-logo delivery device

Also reject when:

* front side is unclear
* operation is unclear
* silhouette is weak
* glow dominates materials
* concept art cannot survive runtime reduction
* asset only looks convincing at 1000+ px

---

# 11. Anchor Example: Field Fabricator Mk1

The Field Fabricator Mk1 is the anchor implementation of this contract.

Classification: **Class A — Utility Node**

Player read:

> Compact powered compound machinery used to produce deployable infrastructure packages.

It should appear valuable and technologically capable, but not monumental.

---

## 11.1 Locked Field Fabricator Design

### Primary mass

A squat armored **circular / horseshoe fabrication chamber** occupying the center-upper portion of the sprite.

The chamber must visibly contain:

* fabrication cradle or work plate
* one mechanical fabrication arm/tool head
* restrained internal teal illumination

The machine should appear capable of physically constructing compact equipment.

### Front interaction face

Bottom-facing.

Required:

* shallow service ramp / loading lip
* central access lane into chamber
* compact control interface immediately adjacent to the working face

The player should intuitively approach from below.

### Secondary utility mass

One side carries a compact service cluster.

Preferred components:

* single reservoir / power-conditioning cylinder
* thick protected conduit
* one vented housing

Do not mirror every component symmetrically.

### Opposite-side utility mass

Use a quieter rectangular service enclosure containing:

* ventilation
* control hardware
* maintenance panel
* optionally small signal beacon

This side should visually balance the tank without duplicating it.

### Fabrication chamber

The chamber is the visual focal point.

Geometry:

* reinforced partial ring
* inset inner basin
* heavy retaining clamps
* one descending tool head
* small work surface

Teal light should originate mostly **inside** the chamber.

No giant floating holographic cube in normal idle state.

An active fabrication animation may briefly display a restrained process projection.

### Heraldry

Maximum:

* one primary Custodian service seal
* optionally one secondary small marking

Do not use skulls.

Do not use multiple banners.

Do not make the fabricator religious.

---

## 11.2 Field Fabricator Color Target

Body:

* dark blue-gray / charcoal metal

Structural edging:

* subtly lighter cold steel

Trim:

* sparse aged brass / bronze

Interior:

* dark teal-black recess

Operational emission:

* cyan-teal

Hazard markings:

* extremely restrained faded ochre/yellow where mechanically useful

No bright clean yellow-black industrial theme across the whole machine.

---

## 11.3 Field Fabricator Detail Hierarchy

At gameplay scale the player must see, in order:

1. outer silhouette
2. central chamber
3. front ramp/access
4. internal teal operating state
5. one side tank / utility mass
6. small console
7. minor detail

If vents, bolts, cables, logos, or scratches compete with items 1–4, simplify them.

---

# 12. Runtime Asset Specification

Do not use a giant concept-art PNG directly.

Prepare production sprite art separately.

## 12.1 Canonical runtime canvas

Use:

`128 × 96 px`

This corresponds naturally to the existing `4 × 3` 32px footprint.

The actual painted machine should occupy approximately:

* width: 104–116 px
* height: 78–90 px

Leave transparent breathing room around the asset.

## 12.2 Origin

Sprite origin:

`Vector2(64, 48)`

World origin should continue representing the logical center of the existing structure.

Do not offset gameplay placement merely to compensate for the artwork.

# 14. Active Animation Spec

If/when supplied:

`field_fabricator_mk1__active__4f__128x96.png`

Four horizontal frames.

Each frame:

* 128×96

Animation should be subtle.

Frame intent:

### Frame 1

Idle active chamber.
Tool head centered.
Low teal illumination.

### Frame 2

Tool head lowers 1–2 px.
Chamber light intensifies slightly.

### Frame 3

Fabrication pulse.
Small restrained spark/process-light event.
No giant VFX.

### Frame 4

Tool retract begins.
Glow begins returning toward baseline.

Target playback:

`5–7 FPS`

Loop only while actual fabrication service is visibly working, if runtime exposes that state cleanly.

Do not fake activity continuously if the simulation does not expose a real work state.

For first implementation, powered idle art is sufficient.

---

# 13. Artist / Generator Prompt Contract

Any future prompt or brief for Custodian structures should include these constraints:

## Required prompt language

* dark industrial field infrastructure
* top-down 2D game structure
* readable at gameplay scale
* grim fortified utility
* weathered steel and stone-metal composite
* restrained teal/cyan operational lighting
* subtle Custodian insignia
* clear front interaction face
* no shrine dominance
* no glossy generic sci-fi
* transparent background

## Required anti-drift language

* avoid cathedral / altar vibes
* avoid excessive ornament
* avoid cluttered over-detailing
* avoid consumer sci-fi polish
* avoid giant decorative glow
* avoid unclear silhouette

---

# 14. Final Locked Summary

If someone asks, "What is a Custodian structure?" the answer should be:

> A Custodian structure is a fortified industrial machine or installation built by an organized ruined authority. It is weathered, functional, and readable first, with restrained holy-tech undertones, a dark steel-and-stone material language, limited teal operational light, and a clear gameplay-facing interaction side.

And if they ask, "What is a Custodian fabricator?":

> A Custodian fabricator is a compact field-deployable industrial production node: a squat armored machine with a central fabrication chamber, a readable front access face, a small control interface, one or two support modules, restrained signal lighting, and minimal but deliberate faction identity.
